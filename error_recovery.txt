Root Cause 

The root cause is that Groq's free/standard tier has a rate limit on requests-per-minute. 
When you upload say 10 agendas and fire them all off instantly, you blow past that limit fast. 
The fix isn't clever — it's just not sending requests faster than Groq allows, and gracefully waiting when you do overshoot.

Now there are two layers to resolve the rate limit error:

Layer 1 — Prevention (stop hitting the limit)

Frontend waits 2s between each agenda before even sending the next request
Server adds another 1.5s asyncio.sleep after each successful Groq call
Together they space out requests so you rarely hit the limit in the first place

Layer 2 — Recovery (handle it when you do hit it)

Server retries up to 5× with exponential backoff (2→4→8→16→32s)
If the server exhausts its retries, the frontend catches the 429 and retries 3 more times (8→16→24s)
A live countdown banner shows the user exactly what's happening during waits

If you keep hitting limits even with these delays, you have two more levers:

Increase INTER_REQUEST_MS in the frontend (e.g. 4000 for 4s gaps)
Increase BASE_BACKOFF in main.py (e.g. 4.0 to start at 4s backoff)
